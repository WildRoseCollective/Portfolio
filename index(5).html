About

Who We Are

The WildROSE Collective is an interdisciplinary team of scholar/artists who are united by a shared experience of urgency in the climate emergency. We believe that the polycrisis of the Anthropocene demands forms of knowing and making that are sited, grounded, and feminist — attending to the particular places, histories, and bodies that environmental crisis presses upon. We fuse poetics with physics, botany and data science, citizen science and critical theory, always in service of more just relations with the living world.

Our interdisciplinary work together over past projects including with the award-winning Decameron Collective has spanned VR installations, sound art, documentary film, projection and installation art, all followed by rigorous peer-reviewed scholarship. We are committed to training the next generation of environmental humanities researchers while building durable public partnerships.



Affiliated institutions include Toronto Metropolitan University, Athabasca University, Ontario Tech University, University of Waterloo, York University, and the Canadian Research Knowledge Network.

Monique Tschofen

Co-Principal Investigator

Toronto Metropolitan University · English

Monique Tschofen is an interdisciplinary scholar, digital storyteller, and filmmaker whose work spans Canadian literature and culture, feminist and queer theory, environmental humanities, and digital media. With other members of the WildROSE Collective and the Decameron Collective, she has developed award-winning research-creation projects at the intersection of technology, ecology, and art. Monique leads the Wildrose Collective's theoretical and methodological innovations in feminist ecological research-creation.

Jolene Armstrong researches Indigenous literature, environmental justice, and the intersections of colonialism and ecology in Northern Alberta. Objective 1Epistemological Innovation

Inventing and testing new aesthetic and expressive forms — sound, AR/VR, immersive installations — that make pressing symptoms of the Anthropocene open to deep consideration.

Objective 2Methodological Development

Marrying historical and ecological analysis with artistic experimentation, theorizing how sited feminist ecological research-creation generates radical insight in times of emergency.

Objective 3Pedagogical Innovation

Working closely with HQP to cultivate professional interdisciplinary skills in environmental humanities, research-creation, and digital stewardship.

Objective 4Public Engagement

Building lasting partnerships among Canadian universities, cultural institutions, archives, and global networks while demonstrating the value of the humanities for engaging with environmental crisis.

Jolene Armstrong & Monique Tschofen

Water Songs: Documenting the Imperceptible

Using ecoutic, contact, and aquatic microphones and microscopes, this project listens in on the stories that tree trunks, bark, moss, water, dirt, and stones have to tell. By making the imperceptible perceivable, it proposes a lexicon of understanding that exists outside of but alongside human language — attuning us to the livingness of everything around us.

Monique Tschofen & Jolene Armstrong · ELO 2025

Fire and Water

A diptych installation exhibited at ELO 2025. Tschofen's kinetic poetry — generated by a Markov model trained on the New York Times live feed of the Palisades Fire and rendered over watercolors of viral fire footage — meets Armstrong's sculpture of water resting on an encyclopedia entry on erosion: what isn't burning in a world on fire is flooding. Together the works address the climate crisis, disaster media, and thirsty AIs.

Jolene Armstrong & Monique Tschofen · 2025

Solastalgia

An interactive documentary anchored in theories of embodiment — asking what our bodies already know about climate change. Drawing on Albrecht's concept of solastalgia (the distress caused by environmental change) and Walter Benjamin's Passagenwerk, this intertextual Scalar assemblage weaves bookmaking, watercoloring, embodied filmmaking, and augmented reality into a living, data-driven response to the polycrisis.

Monique Tschofen · Sound: Hendrick de Haan · 2025

Aquaphoria

A poetic environmental documentary in five chapters tracing the filmmaker's encounters with water — from the bubbling rivers of Asheville, North Carolina to the Californian seashore to the fjords and glaciers of Alaska. At once ecstatic and grieving, Aquaphoria argues that we need new vocabularies to capture the intimate scales of the climate emergency.
